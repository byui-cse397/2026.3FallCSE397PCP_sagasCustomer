## Marking invented material

Any content that a reader could mistake for a real place, person, quote, record,
or testimony must be marked beside that content:

- **Illustrative example — invented for this draft** for an illustration or
  sample place.
- **Invented sample passage — not a real account** for a fabricated quotation
  or passage.
- **Invented contributor — not a real person** for a fabricated contributor.

Labels must be visible text. Do not rely on colour, italics, icons, hover text,
tooltips, or the page-level banner alone. The current draft contains no
invented quote, person, place, date, or testimony; only the illustration area
needs the point-of-use label.

## Copy decisions

- “Every place has more than one story” is the central idea and avoids claiming
  that any single account is complete.
- “What is remembered” leaves room for memory, records, and later public
  material without promising a specific source type.
- “Claims beneath it” introduces the product’s distinctive reading model without
  using technical contract terms.
- “Nothing is quietly settled” communicates that disagreement remains visible.
- No popularity, score, agreement count, or ranking is mentioned.
- No invented person, place, date, or quote appears in the copy.
